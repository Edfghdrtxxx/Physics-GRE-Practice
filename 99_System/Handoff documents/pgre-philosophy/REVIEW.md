# REVIEW — `docs/Project Docs/PGRE-Philosophy.md`

**Verdict: SHIP_WITH_NITS**

The document is the right genre. Headings are philosophical, CPG questions are not used as sources, era tensions from the memos are kept rather than flattened, and an agent that reads only this file can reject a textbook clone. Remaining issues are local: a handful of illustrations still point at identifiable released items, and the failed-theory / cognitive-move passages will be copied as recipes unless they are further compressed. Neither requires a new spine.

---

## Findings

### 1. Identifiable released-item illustrations (spoiler + clone-bait)

**Severity:** major

**Evidence.** No ETS stem, choice list, or figure is quoted. Several one-line illustrations are still specific enough that a person who has sat a released form would recognize the item, and a later generator would be tempted to restage it.

In **Costume is not content**, the superconductor costume is not left at the class level. It names the undergraduate collapse (tangential $B$ just outside, from $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0$) *and* the sophisticated neighbor (microscopic pairing in the choices). That pairing of slogan plus named wrong neighbor is the signature of one recent-era specialized item (the same item appears on the hundred-item parent and on the current practice book, which this document correctly calls a deletion-subset). The same section’s elementary counterpart, “buoyancy that depends on displaced volume rather than orientation,” names the exact unused-given trap of one recent-era mechanics item.

Elsewhere the same recent-era wave-matching item is sketched twice without being named: **The expensive look and the cheap door** gives “a matching condition plus an obvious bound such as $0\le R\le 1$”; **What the choices are for** gives “a coefficient that must be 0 when two sides match and 1 when a step is infinite.” **A figure is an argument** and the lab paragraph give “precision versus accuracy as histogram shape,” which is the signature of one middle-form laboratory item, not a class of lab items. **What the stem does** plants “an instrument gain when the asked quantity is a phase,” which is the unused-knob of one middle-form oscilloscope item.

These are not copyright dumps. They are stem-identifiable paraphrases of *tested claims and distractors*, which is enough to spoil those items and enough to turn the philosophy into a tiny clone deck.

**Fix.** Speak one level up. Superconductor → “an advanced condensed-matter costume that collapses to a Maxwell boundary condition, with the microscopic mechanism sitting as a sophisticated neighbor.” Buoyancy → “an elementary statics costume whose unused geometric detail is the trap.” $R$-bounds → “a boundary-value costume whose legal range of a coefficient already kills three choices.” Histogram / unused gain → “lab as reading a representation: shape of a distribution, an unused instrument knob.” One illustration per principle, and never slogan-plus-that-item’s-distractor.

### 2. Failed-theory families and cognitive-move lists will be used as a template deck

**Severity:** major

**Evidence.** The opening of the document forbids this: “Do not treat the moves named below as a menu. Treat them as evidence of a single craft.” **How to know you have written one** repeats the warning with the Atwood test. The headings themselves are not a catalog.

The body still offers two scannable decks. **What the choices are for** restates Kahn’s six scan-patterns (dimensions, decades, factor of two, limits, interior numerical factors, unrelated intermediates) and then a comma-list of recurring failed theories (wrong scaling, conjugate pair with $L$/$H$ and $\gamma$/$\gamma-1$ and $|c|^2$, forgotten two, wrong region, classification, units veto, sign, Galilean surrogate, zero/nonzero, intermediate-as-final). **One cognitive move, then stop** then lists recognition, limiting case and symmetry, conservation as selector, units as first move, binomial approximation, one substitution. A later agent will not treat those as evidence of one craft. It will write to the list: one conjugate-pair item, one units-veto item, one “immediately after” jump item.

That is not the topic encyclopedia the user rejected, and it is not “Archetype 1: Atwood.” It is the neighboring failure mode: a *move* catalog. The source memos needed those families as evidence. The standing philosophy needs fewer of them.

**Fix.** Keep the principle in one sentence: each wrong option is the output of a nearby wrong theory a hurried physicist would actually run; the right option is often the unique survivor of a filter the stem did not request. Give two unnamed examples in running prose, not ten labeled families. Keep Kahn’s scan order only as a claim about *what an item is* (choices were built so that the cheapest filter is a legal path), not as a six-step recipe. Let **How to know you have written one** carry the operational load (“do the four wrong choices name failed theories, or are they a spread around a number?”).

### 3. Audit section rejects textbook clones; generation is still anchored to named doors

**Severity:** nit (operationality)

**Evidence.** **How to know you have written one** is actually usable as a rejector. The tests map onto the success criteria: homework-grader algebra, unused-given filter, theatrical setup with a cheap door, autopsied distractors, second route through limit/units/symmetry, graduate costume collapsing to an undergraduate slogan, refusal keys, figure-as-argument, hundred-second no-calculator arithmetic, equal-value mixed difficulty, no formula scoring. The closing paragraph is a generation order: name the principle, stage just enough world, withhold the procedure, leave bait, plant four autopsies, make the cheap door visible only after the slogan, sometimes let nothing happen, stop.

A generator that passed those tests could still fail to *invent* a GRE item. The document does not say how to specify a figure that is the given rather than decoration; how to choose numbers that snap without copying Pythagorean $\gamma$ triples; what fraction of a short drill should never want a number; when a factoid/recognition item should appear (it says they are part of the voice, then never audits for them); when a shared-stem cluster is worth the reading tax; when “most nearly” or a Roman-numeral bundle is the right grammar. **One cognitive move, then stop** and **The expensive look and the cheap door** teach the aesthetic mostly by example, which pushes the agent back toward finding 2’s lists and finding 1’s identifiable setups.

That is an acceptable gap for a philosophy — it is not a how-to manual — but it is the gap that will show up first in generated drills.

**Fix.** Optional, and only after findings 1–2. Add three *method* sentences, not three templates: (i) specify the figure first when the item is geometric or data-shaped, then write the shortest stem that the figure does not already state; (ii) pick numbers so the right identity lands on an integer or a small ratio, and treat ugly arithmetic as evidence the identity is wrong; (iii) in any sitting-length set, include some items that never want a number, some that are pure recognition, and at least one refusal key. Do not add a density table.

### 4. Coverage of the source memos is good; a few truths were dropped or slightly over-extended

**Severity:** nit

**Evidence.** The tensions the sources refused to flatten are in the document, in the right register:

- Freshman-text versus Griffiths costume: **Costume is not content** keeps Conway’s “at least 80 percent” freshman book next to ETS’s quantum-plus-atomic quarter, and states the writing rule (costume may be upper-division; demanded performance is not a graduate interior).
- Derive versus recall: **The table of information is not the syllabus** keeps Kahn, Brown/CWRU, departmental pages, and Conway side by side, then the clock-prefers-the-reflex rule.
- Units-as-item versus units-as-check: **What the choices are for** dates the decline (~2000, Kahn) and forbids both a paper of unit-vetoes and a paper where units never kill a choice.
- 2023 format change as duration, not craft: **What the test is measuring** and **Time is a design parameter** give the 1.70 versus 1.71 minutes, ETS’s access/convenience language, and the no-think-time conclusion.
- ets2024 as deletion-subset: stated in the opening section as same stems, choices, and figures, new numbering.
- Calculation versus trick-spotting, older exams as muscle versus style match, napkin-intended versus clock-punishes-derivation versus instruction-no-longer-trains: all held in **One cognitive move**, **The instrument is stable**, and **What a GRE item refuses to be**.

Dropped, and not needed as structure: ETS validity/Kuncel, subscore machinery, KR-20, the official nine-row percentage table (correctly compressed to one coverage paragraph), Griffiths-on-the-committee anecdote, Quora recycling folklore, form-by-form twin templates (positronium, $T^4$, most-probable radius). Those were right to drop.

Dropped with a little cost:

- The 1980s ERIC classification (WEB-RESEARCH §3): GRE items more often recall / standard application / option-list work; classroom tests aimed at novel problem-solving. The philosophy already contrasts homework derivation with GRE discrimination, but it never says the *setting* is usually standard, not novel. That sentence would help a generator refuse researchy vignettes.
- The recent memo’s observation that the thirty deletions were mixed (not “all the easy ones” or “all the hard ones”). The philosophy says the 70-item sitting is not a new species and that difficulty is mixed; it does not explicitly block the misreading “write seventy easy recognitions, because the cut trimmed hardness.”
- Kahn’s “possibly making a comeback” on pure units items was flattened to a one-way decline. Harmless if the writing rule remains “a handful, not a paper.”
- Refusal-item “official percent-correct in the teens to low thirties is typical” (**The refusal**) is a middle-form observation stated as a GRE law. Vintage and recent forms also have a hard tail; the number is more precise than the sources allow as a universal.

**Fix.** One sentence in **What a GRE item refuses to be** or **Costume is not content**: the setting is almost always a standard undergraduate situation asked sideways, not a novel problem. One clause in the format paragraph: the deletions that made the current booklet were mixed in kind, not a hardness cut. Hedge the refusal P+ as “on released forms, often the hard tail,” not “typical.”

### 7. Factual caution is mostly held; a few project measurements are stated as GRE facts

**Severity:** nit

**Evidence.** Committee intent is not invented for the 2023 cut: ETS’s access/convenience language is used, and the deletion-subset claim is observational. Conway is the source for short/to-the-point, six-level review, easy arithmetic, conceptual and dimensional paths, freshman-text 80 percent, and the pressure/memorization concession. Guessing advice is date-stamped for October 2026. Wittman’s “nail about 40” is correctly rescaled (40/70 as roughly the 43rd percentile under the current conversion, not a high-score target).

Project-bank measurements are stated in the narrator’s voice as properties of the exam: later-form median stem length “under thirty words”; “which of the following” as “about half” of later stems; figures on “roughly three items in ten” later and “about a third” middle; vintage stems “forty or fifty.” Those match FINDINGS-recent / vintage / middle. They are not ETS-published specifications. “Questions are recycled often, which is why so few exams have been released” is Kahn, presented as fact; WEB-RESEARCH treated exact recycle counts as folklore, but the qualitative production claim is sourced.

**Fix.** One hedge on the observational counts: “on the later released forms, once markup is stripped…” so a generator does not treat “under thirty words” as an ETS rule. Leave the Kahn recycling sentence; do not add a number.

### 8. Voice, length, repo rules, self-containment

**Severity:** nit

**Evidence.** About 6,050 words. Dense, but it is one briefing, not an encyclopedia, and the first draft was already cut. No emojis. Math is in LaTeX (`$\gamma$`, `$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0$`, `$\tfrac12 kT$`, $|c|^2$). Public voices are named (ETS, Conway, Kahn and Anderson, Brown, Cheong, Levy, Physics Forums, CWRU). Internal handoff filenames are not cited. The opening sentence states the job: an agent should read this and nothing else, tell a GRE item from a textbook problem, and generate the former.

Minor voice nits: UVA SPS’s “the content of the exam itself is not very hard, but doing well requires that you know it perfectly” is absorbed into **The table of information is not the syllabus** without attribution, so it reads as narrator/ETS. Levy is name-dropped for a species claim that the surrounding Conway/Kahn/Brown paragraph already makes. October 2026 guessing advice will stale; it belongs, given the sitting this file is aimed at, but it should stay clearly operational rather than philosophical.

**Fix.** Attribute the “know it perfectly / finite item space” sentence to a departmental coaching commonplace, or recast it as a production fact (finite slogans, recycled demands). Optional: drop the Levy name and keep the species sentence.

## What was not thoroughly checked

- **CPG banks were not opened.** Exclusion is judged from the philosophy’s citations and from the absence of chapter-problem residue in the prose, not from a CPG-to-philosophy diff.
- **Not every illustrative clause was matched against every released stem.** High-suspicion phrases (superconductor / pairing, buoyancy / orientation, histogram precision, unused instrument gain, $R$-bounds / infinite step, Schwarzschild / compact object) were keyword-checked in `content/bank/ets-exams.js` for uniqueness of the underlying item. Hits are reported above only by *kind*. The vintage drill pool (`PGRE.ETS_DRILLS`, GR8677 and GR9277) was not searched.
- **Stem-length, figure-rate, and “which of the following” fractions were not recounted.** They were checked for consistency with the three FINDINGS memos, not recomputed from the bank.
- **Quotations were not re-fetched from Conway, Kahn, ETS PDFs, or departmental pages.** They were checked against WEB-RESEARCH’s extracts.
- **Operationality was not trial-generated.** No items were written from this document alone. Claims about what a later agent would copy are inferences from list shape, not a generation test.
- **Percentile arithmetic** (40/70 ≈ 43rd) was checked against WEB-RESEARCH’s ETS table excerpts, not against a freshly opened *Guide to the Use of Scores*.

Do not treat silence on a stem as a finding of absence.
